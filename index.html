You are not fighting a lack of willpower. You are running a learned pattern.

Learned patterns can be understood, and what can be understood can be changed. This is a free framework for naming the pattern you are in and climbing out of it — one rung at a time.

Start where you are

Read this first

This is a self-understanding tool, not treatment. It does not diagnose, and it does not replace therapy, medical care, or detox.

If you are facing physical withdrawal from alcohol, benzodiazepines, or opioids, that can be medically dangerous — talk to a doctor before stopping. If you are thinking about harming yourself, please reach a person tonight: in the US call or text 988; the SAMHSA helpline is 1-800-662-4357, free and open around the clock. Outside the US, findahelpline.com lists local lines.

Rung 0 is safety. It always comes first.

Where are you right now?

Pick the one that sounds like today

You don't need to read anything first. Find the line that fits, and you'll get one question answered and one thing to do in the next ten minutes.

Why can't I think clearly right now?

Because you're flooded, and nobody sees themselves accurately in that state. This isn't a character problem — it's your nervous system doing what it's built to do. Insight is not available yet, and chasing it now usually makes things louder.

Nothing else on this page matters until this settles. Come back to the rest later.

Do this now

Put both feet flat on the floor. Breathe out longer than you breathe in — four in, six out — six times. Then name five things you can see in the room, out loud. That's the whole task.

Why do I keep repeating something I've decided to stop?

Because it was learned, not chosen. It installed itself through repetition until it ran without being called — which is why it arrives before you decide anything. Understanding it doesn't dissolve it, but seeing it clearly is where every other move starts.

Right now you're trying to think your way out of something you've never actually watched. So watch it first.

Do this now

Write down three moments from today: what set it off, what you felt, what you wanted to do, what you did. Don't judge any of it and don't fix anything. You're collecting data, not confessing.

Am I the problem?

No. You're the one noticing the problem, and those are not the same thing. If the pattern were identical to you, there'd be nobody left over to be worried about it — and yet here you are, worried.

That gap between the noticer and the thing being noticed is small and it's real. Everything from here works by widening it.

Do this now

Say this out loud, once, even if it feels false: "This is a pattern running through my nervous system. It is not who I am." It's not supposed to feel true yet. Saying it is the work.

Where does this energy go now?

Somewhere physical, and quickly. Bracing against an urge keeps it at the centre of your attention, which is exactly where it wants to be — that's why gritting your teeth works for ten minutes and then stops working. The energy is real and it isn't going to evaporate. It has to go somewhere.

Don't try to want it less. Move it.

Do this now

Pick one and start inside the next minute: walk to the end of the street, cold water on your hands and face, twenty push-ups, or make something badly. Motion, not stillness. If you did this, you did the whole job for today — nothing here is unfinished.

Below this line the work changes — these two are for a calm day, not a hard moment

What replaces the old pattern?

A container — something outside your willpower that keeps working on the days you're not at your best. Willpower is a bad load-bearing wall. Structure isn't.

This is cold work. Do it when nothing is firing, because designing your life mid-surge doesn't go well.

Do this week

Build exactly one, and make it small enough that it survives a bad week: a place in your home that's for one thing only, fifteen minutes a day that belong to you, or one clear rule about who gets access to your energy and when.

Who am I now?

The one who chooses. Triggers still show up — that doesn't stop, and it isn't a relapse. What changed is that they arrive as information rather than as instructions.

The last piece isn't more work on yourself. It's being witnessed, and eventually witnessing someone else. A pattern isolates by design; the exit is other people.

Do this now

Tell one person something true about where you've been. Not a performance and not the whole story — one true sentence to one human being who gets to know you.

How to use this

Read before you start

What it's for

Any pattern that keeps running after you've decided to stop it. A substance, a scroll, a relationship you keep re-entering, a temper, a spending habit, a spiral you know by heart. You don't need a diagnosis and you don't need to call yourself anything.

"Why do I keep doing this?" · "I know better and I still can't stop." · "I don't want to be this way anymore."

How much to tell it

Less than you think. The framework runs on what's happening now — the trigger, the feeling, the urge, what you did next. That's the whole first move.

When it does reach backward, it's looking for a category, not a story: was what was missing safety, mirroring, co-regulation, a container? You can answer that in four words. You never have to narrate your life to get the benefit.

Share what's useful to you. Nothing here rewards disclosing more, and no one is owed your history.

Before you paste it into an AI

This website stores nothing. No account, no analytics, no cookies, no log of who you are or what you took.

An AI assistant is a different matter. If you paste the primer into ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, or anything similar, that conversation is stored on that company's servers. It may be reviewed by people. It may be used to train their systems. It can be subject to a subpoena. That is true of every one of them, including the one you may be reading this through.

So: use a temporary or incognito chat mode if the service offers one, and turn off training in its settings if you can. You never need real names — not yours, and especially not anyone else's. "Someone close to me" works exactly as well as a name. Skip your employer, your town, and anything that identifies a third party who didn't choose this.

None of this means don't use it. It means go in knowing where your words land.

When you're activated

Reaching for this instead of the pattern is itself the move — that's Rung 3, and it counts.

But know what it will do when you arrive keyed up: it will ground you, not analyze you. Rung 0 comes first for a reason. Nobody sees their own pattern clearly while flooded, and trying to do insight work mid-surge usually makes the surge louder. Breath, body, the present minute. The understanding keeps until you're steady enough to hold it.

It is not a person

This can help you put something down at 3am. It cannot know you, and it should never become the only place you say true things.

A pattern isolates by design — that's not incidental to it, it's how it survives. Anything that becomes your one outlet is feeding it, however well it listens. Use this to get steady, then take what you found to a person: a friend, a group, a counsellor, a meeting, someone walking the same road.

The framework's own rule, and it isn't optional: never let this stand in for human connection.

What it isn't

Not treatment, not diagnosis, not therapy, not crisis support. If you're facing physical withdrawal from alcohol, benzodiazepines, or opioids, talk to a doctor before stopping — that one can be dangerous. If you're thinking about harming yourself, call or text 988 in the US, or find your local line at findahelpline.com.

The seam — the work changes here

Rungs 0–3 are acute. You do them while the pattern is firing. The urge supplies the momentum and every step has an obvious move. You're responding — you never have to work out where you are.

Rungs 4–6 are cold. You do them on an ordinary day when nothing is happening. Nothing pushes you forward, so you have to generate the direction yourself, and that means stopping to take stock.

Stopping at rung 3 is a complete intervention, not an abandoned climb. You named it, stepped out of it, moved the energy. That's the whole job for that moment. Don't try to design new structures mid-surge — that's the Rung 0 error arriving from the other end. And if the flow goes dead around rung 4: the tool changed modes. You didn't fail.
